1. What Is HelixHack?

HelixHack is Silicon Circle's inaugural teen innovation hackathon, held in Chennai in October 2026. It is a city-wide, team-based event that challenges students from Grade 8 to Grade 12 to design and build solutions to real-world problems across four themes, using science, technology, engineering, and mathematics (STEM) as their foundation.

Teams participate in one of two categories: Pitch-Only (ideation and research-based) or Prototype (working model, app, hardware, or software). The event spans online qualifying rounds followed by an offline finale demo day held during the Navratri school holidays on Saturday, October 18, 2026.

Why Silicon Circle Is Organising This

Most hackathons available to teens in Chennai are too senior in scope, too niche, or not locally accessible. HelixHack fills that gap by creating a structured, mentored, and genuinely rigorous competition designed specifically for school-age innovators. Silicon Circle is a community. We believe students do not need to wait until they graduate before making meaningful contributions to real problems.

2. Themes

All teams must build their project within one of the four themes below. Themes are intentionally broad to allow creative interpretation, while focused enough to give teams a clear problem space to work within.

Theme 1: AI & Emerging Technology

Projects that use, explore, or critically examine artificial intelligence, machine learning, automation, computer vision, robotics, or other cutting-edge technologies to solve a real-world problem.

Example problems: AI tools that identify struggling students early; automated waste sorting systems; local-language AI assistants for non-English speakers in Chennai.

Theme 2: Sustainability & Climate

Projects focused on environmental preservation, renewable energy, climate resilience, food security, water conservation, or building a more sustainable future for urban communities.

Example problems: Rooftop rainwater harvesting systems for Chennai apartments; apps that track a school's carbon footprint; biodegradable alternatives to single-use plastics.

Theme 3: Mental Health, Student Wellbeing & EdTech

Projects that address mental health challenges faced by students, or that use technology to improve how young people learn, study, and grow. This theme bridges emotional wellbeing with educational innovation.

Example problems: A peer-support platform for school students; a smart study scheduler that accounts for stress and burnout; gamified revision tools built for board exam pressure.

Theme 4: Accessibility & Inclusion

Projects that remove barriers for people with disabilities, language differences, economic disadvantages, or other challenges that limit their full participation in society, education, or technology.

Example problems: Sign-language translation tools; low-cost assistive devices for visually impaired students; simplified apps for first-generation smartphone users.

Category Breakdown

Pitch-Only Category

Teams develop a well-researched, evidence-backed idea for solving a real problem. No working prototype is required. The emphasis is on depth of thinking, scientific rationale, feasibility, and presentation skills.

  • Finale Format: Live stage presentation in fixed scheduled slots.
  • Time per team: 5-minute pitch + 3-minute Q&A from judging panel.
  • What to bring: Slide deck (main screen), printed references/materials.
  • Judged on: Problem framing, depth of research, STEM basis, feasibility, clarity.
Prototype Category

Teams build a working implementation (software, hardware device, physical model, web or mobile app). Must demonstrate the solution in action live at the stall.

  • Finale Format: Stall-based expo hall with rotating judge visits.
  • Time per team: 8–10 min per judge visit (5-min demo + 3-5 min Q&A).
  • What to bring: Live prototype, project poster, data visualizations, stall decorations.
  • Judged on: All Pitch-Only criteria + technical implementation quality and sophistication.

4. Vibe Coding and AI Tool Policy

The use of AI-assisted coding and development tools is permitted in HelixHack. This includes tools such as GitHub Copilot, Cursor, ChatGPT, Claude, v0, Replit AI, and any similar AI pair-programming or code-generation assistant.

Core Principle: AI is a tool, not a team member. Teams are evaluated on their understanding and application of technology, not on their ability to prompt a model. One rule applies without exception: every team member must be able to fully explain their code, logic, design decisions, and implementation to judges at any point during the event.

✅ Permitted Use

  • Using AI tools to generate, assist, debug, or refactor code; scaffold boilerplate; generate design assets; or summarise background research.
  • Using AI-generated code or components, provided the team fully understands what the code does and why.

❌ Not Permitted Use

  • Using AI to generate the core problem identification or innovation idea. The insight, framing, and direction must originate from the team.
  • Submitting AI-generated work that team members cannot explain. This is treated as academic dishonesty and results in disqualification.

💡 Judge Expectation: At the finale, judges will ask teams to walk through sections of their code or hardware logic directly. Inability to answer clearly will result in score deductions under the STEM Integration and Presentation criteria.

11. Discord Daily Progress Protocol

During the Build Period, all finalist teams are required to post daily progress updates in the Silicon Circle Discord server's dedicated finalist channel. This requirement exists for three reasons: accountability, community energy, and fuelling the People's Choice vote.

What Each Update Must Include

  • One or more photos showing physical or digital progress made that day.
  • A written typeout of at least 3-5 sentences covering: what was done today, what worked, what didn't work, and what is next.

Rules & Integration

  • Frequency: One update per day. Missed days reduce bonus score (up to +5 pts).
  • Authenticity: Must reflect genuine progress. Copying previous posts is penalized.
  • People's Choice: Silicon Circle reshares build highlights on Instagram & LinkedIn.

12. STEM Integration Framework

HelixHack is not just a technology competition. It is a STEM event. Every project, regardless of theme or category, must demonstrate grounding in science, technology, engineering, or mathematics.

Encouraged Approaches

  • Using real-world data or publicly available datasets to validate a problem.
  • Building hardware prototypes using Arduino, Raspberry Pi, or sensors.
  • Applying mathematical modelling, simulation, or statistical analysis.
  • Conducting a structured mini-experiment to test feasibility.

STEM Award

The Best STEM Implementation award is given specifically to the team whose project most rigorously and creatively applies STEM principles, whether through mathematics, physical engineering, scientific experimentation, or code.
